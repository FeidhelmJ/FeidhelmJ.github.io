L.

How can Lobster Land use this model? Write a couple paragraphs’ worth of thoughts assessing how Lobster Land might apply the results. You could explore “what if” scenarios, or how the park’s priorities might shape their decisions

The k-means clustering analysis gives Lobster Land a clearer picture of how different kinds of operational days naturally group together based on real park data. Because all variables were standardized using z-scores before modeling, no single metric—such as revenue, attendance, or temperature—was allowed to dominate the results. Each factor carried equal weight, allowing the algorithm to uncover patterns that reflect the overall shape of a day’s performance rather than the scale of its numbers.

By mapping clusters to days of the week or months of the season, the park can forecast what kind of day it’s heading into and prepare accordingly. For instance, if Cluster 3 is mostly made up of weekend days with large crowds, the operations team can plan more entertainment offerings or open additional food stalls. Similarly, if Cluster 1 corresponds to cooler, lower-attendance weekdays, marketing could run mid-week ticket promotions to boost turnout. Days in clusters characterized by high visitor counts and above-average revenue might call for increased staff levels, extended park hours, and higher inventory in dining or merchandise areas. Conversely, clusters with fewer visitors but higher per-capita spending might suggest an opportunity to focus on premium experiences or targeted marketing to passholders and tourists.

Overall, this model helps Lobster Land make decisions that are both data-driven and proactive. Instead of reacting to crowds after they arrive, the park can anticipate demand, allocate resources efficiently, and design strategies tailored to each of the days that fall into different cluster's behavior pattern.

D.

It might seem tempting here to just skip the model-building step – instead of building a model, we could just rank the bundles by average rating, in descending order, and then just tell Lobster Land to implement all the features in the highest-rated bundle.

What would we miss by doing this, though? What value does a conjoint analysis model provide, which we would not have if we simply ranked the bundles?

  • Conjoint analysis delivers insights about particular levels and particular attributes. It teaches us about the sorts of things that make products more appealing to less appealing to consumers
  • Bundles are combinations of multiple features. If you just rank bundles, you don’t know which features are driving high ratings, this is essential when analyzing variables like crowding index where there are four options respondents can choose from.
  • If you just average ratings, you’re limited to the bundles that were shown. Conjoint analysis builds a model that can help management predict customer preferences for new, untested bundles by assessing the coefficeint values of the current model. For example, below I will highlight that respondents really did not like the unlimited re-entry option, therefore Lobster Land can make this adjustment to remove this option for any future testing or for the event itself.

Headliner:

For the headliner options, the base coefficient that was chosen here was the Characacter Parade which was kid-themed. It was the most poorly rated coefficient out of the four given options however it fell just behind the Drone Light Show & Fireworks headliners. It is unsurpising that the Character Parade was the lowest rated headliner as it catered almost exclusively to children. It is also unsurprising to see that the Live Musical was the highest rated coefficient as a live musical caters towards people of all ages, demographics & backgrounds.

Music Atmosphere:

Between the two options for music atmosphere, the Live Classical Orchestra was receieved much better than the Pop Soundtrack which was recorded. There seems to be a trend forming among park visitors preferences with a live performance again being the most preffered option which is something to note for management.

Show Length:

For the show length, the base coefficient that was chosen here was 30 minutes and this was very poorly rated among visitors when compared to the other options. 110 minutes was the most preferred option here with visitors being mostly indifferent about whether they preffered a show of 70 or 150 minutes in length. Personally these values make sense to me, 30 minutes is far too short for a show length given there is more than likely going to be a cost to get in. 110 minutes seems to be long enough that people will get their money's worth, but not too long that they become bored. Management should conduct another survey surrounding show length, remove 30 minutes as an option to try and differentiate between the 70 & 150 minute options.

Peak DB Level:

65 was selected as the base coefficient here. Unsurprisingly, 105 scored terribly among the 3 options with db levels of 65 & 85 scoring almost identically, for Lobster Land management, its evident they should keep db levels below 90.

Crowding Index:

40 was selected as the base coefficient for crowding index and it scored the lowest out of the 4 options. Respondents seem to prefer a crowding index of somewhere between 60 - 80 whilst they did not like the overly busy index of 100. The low score of 100 is expected as in general people just do not like large crowds! I am curious to know however would this coefficient have scored better pre pandemic. 40 scoring low is also unsurprising as although yes, people do not want major crowds, there needs to be enough people present to create a fun and buzzing atmosphere!

Viewing Zone

There isn't much to report for viewing zone aside from the respondents tended to prefer the boardwalk, castle courtyard & the front plaza equally when compared against the sandy beach viewing zone. This is to be expected as the weather will be slightly too chilly this time of year to be spending time on the beach. If a show is to be ran over the summer months visitors opinion may differ here but in my opinion the management team should remove this option and get visitors preferences between the remaining three viewing zones.

Dining Bundle

Again, not a huge amount to report here with visitors being indifferent regarding the four dining bundle options, coefficient values did not stray further from 0.01 from the base.

Re-Entry

In terms of re-entry, visitors felt positively about being able to re-enter the show once, which is likely to accomodate people's bathroom needs. On the flip side they felt really strongly against unlimited re-entries. This makes sense because if people are trying to sit down and enjoy a show which they've paid an admission fee for, the last thing they want is a constant flow of people coming and going, potentially obstructing their view.

Live Host

I would recommend Lobster Land to hire a live host for the event as apposed to an automated voice. Again, following on from the theme earlier of the live musical & atmosphere, the respondents answered very positively about a live emmcee, a good emmcee can make or break any event!

Charity Tie-in

The respondents defintely leaned more towards having a charity tie in with the event as apposed to not having one. I personally think this would be a fantastic idea for the Lobster Land management team to implement. Given the summer months are over, it is likely that the majority of the visitors to the park for these events will be locals & passholders, it would be a fantastic initiative for the park to support a local charity each night and potentially even give a % of the revenue from the gate.
