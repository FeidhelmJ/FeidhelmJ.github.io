Conduct a statistical test to check whether the user_spend variable is normally distributed.

a. What is your test’s null hypothesis?

  • The user spend variable is normally distributed.

b. Based on the p-value that you see as a result, what can you say about the distribution of this variable?

  • p-value: 0.9323
  • The p-value is greater than 0.05, so we fail to reject the null hypothesis, therefore the user spend varible is normally distributed. However, since the sample size greatly exceeds the recommended limit for the Shapiro–Wilk test, the p-value may not be fully reliable, but it is safe to assume is would still remain >0.05. Use a binomial z-test to compare retention_1 between the two versions of the game.

a. What will be the null hypothesis of your test?

  • The proportion of users retained will be the same for both versions of the game one day after download.

b. Next, pick either version (Gulf of Maine or North Atlantic).

  • North Atlantic

c. Then, find the number of users of your chosen version who stayed with the game for one day.

  • 20,119 users stayed with the game for one day.

d. Next, find the expected number of users who would have stayed with the game for one day, if your null hypothesis were true.

i. What is this expected value? How did you arrive at this value?

  • Expected Value = 20,252 users when rounded.

  • To arrive at this value we first had to calculate the mean retention rate for both of the app versions together. From our retention comparison above, we would expect this value to fall somewhere between Gulf of Maine's retention rate of 0.448188, and North Atlantic's rate of 0.442283. We applied this new shared retention rate of 0.445209 to actual value of 20,119 users to arrive at this expected value of 20,252 users.

e. Now, conduct a binomial z-test to determine whether the retention_1 for the version you chose is meaningfully different from the expected number, given your null hypothesis.

  • P-value: 0.21 (rounded).
  • Z-statistic: 1.78 (rounded).

f. Based on the test, what will you conclude?

  • Given that the p-value from our binomial z-test is greater than 0.05, we fail to reject the Null Hypothesis. We can conclude that North Atlantic Day 1 retention is not meaningfully different from the expected retention under the null.
  • The Z-statistic tells us that the North Atlantic retention rate after one day is 1.78 standard errors above the expected rate.

H.

Write a couple sentences of thoughts and/or recommendations for Lobster Land based on the overall results that you have found from the Super Lobster Run data and the statistical tests. Based on what you’ve learned here, what can you say or recommend? Furthermore, what are some other things that you might want to know in order to put these results into context?

Having analyzed all the results from the raw data and the statistical tests it is tough to make a case for one version of the game over another. If anything unfortunately I would recommend the management at Lobster Land to go back to the drawing board!

Looking at the sum_gamerounds variable, the data is almost identical. Earlier I created a filter to remove an outlier where a player had apparently played almost 50,000 round of the Gulf of Maine version but had only a corresponding user spend of 13 dollars so this was likely an impossible value. The median values are similar at 16/17 rounds each respectively, I felt this was a better metric to utilize as both versions of the game saw many users play upwards of 1000 rounds which was causing the mean to be inflated. What is concerning for management is that there were roughly 2000 users for both versions of the game who didn't play a single round.To mediate this they could offer a promotion to users of once they complete 10 rounds of the game, they would be entered into a raffle for a free weekly pass to Lobster Land for the upcoming season.

I would recommend the management to try and implement some incentives to keep players playing the game for longer. It is a worrying statistic that of the 90,000 users that downloaded the app, over half stopped playing after a single round. They could copy the strategy used by online betting providers. This could come in the form of daily spins for small prizes or boosts and even a weekly bonus for consecutive days logged in playing game. To boost user spend, they could also copy part of the 'Fortnite' business model, whereby players are constantly advertised the latest 'skins' that their in-game character can wear. These can be achieved through one-off purchases or through seasonal game passes players can buy and level up.

Management could also develop the app as a whole and take it outside the realm of being just a game. In the off season, the app could provide users with updates on the park, when its due to be open, plans for the season ahead and even run promotional events through the app to win guest passes/seasonal passes for the year ahead. During the season the app could provide users with daily updates on events that are happening, and promotions that the park are running on a week to week basis.

To put these results in broader context, it would be valuable to know how retention varies by player demographics, playtime, or spending tiers, and whether any marketing campaigns were rolled out in the lead up to the release of the app. Combining metrics with demographic and user spend data would help pinpoint why the Gulf of Maine versions performed slightly better and guide future optimization decisions.
