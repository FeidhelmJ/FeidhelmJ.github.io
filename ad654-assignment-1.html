H. A ‘Date’ variable type.

Use the info() function to determine the data type for the ‘Date’ variable.

What type of variable does pandas see it as, currently? Convert this variable into a datetime object, and show that the conversion was successful.

  • Pandas currently sees this variable as an object.

What impact could this have on the variable? (In other words, what is the point of having Python ‘see’ that your date variable is truly a date?)

  • Python will store your dates as true date time objects and not as strings which is important so that data is stored chronologically and not alphabetically.
  • It's important to have your date variable 'seen' as a date when grouping or filtering data. If you want to group revenue data week to week or month to month, you must have your variable 'seen' as a datetime object.

Create a faceted bar plot of food & beverage revenue, with facets for weather type, and bars representing day of the week. Do not include error bars. Be sure to give your plot a clear, descriptive title.

What looks interesting here? Are there certain weather/day combinations that stand out? What’s one important limitation of this visualization?

  • It's unfair to try and create a relationship between people's spending habits and the weather as this is an uncontrollable variable. For example, of course revenue for food and beverage is going to be higher on cloudy days when compared against days where there are thunderstorms or showers due to the surplus of people attending the park.

  • A more accurate comparison may be between weather types and gate revenue.

  • The sample size is also low to make a fair assumption on revenue and weather type. For example we have no data for revenue on Monday's when the weather type was 'showers'. Similarily, on the weekends we have no date for revenue when the weather type was 'thunderstorms'. We don't know was it a case of there was simply no thunderstorms any weekend or did the park close on weekends when thunderstorms were forecasted.
